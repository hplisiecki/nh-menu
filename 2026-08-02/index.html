12:45 Wszystkie nasze strachy Fiction drama loosely based on real events, starring Dawid Ogrodnik as a gay Catholic folk artist and activist in rural Poland who paints murals for his neighbors while facing homophobia in his community.

All Our Fears

12:45–14:15 KNH 4 1h 30m Polska, 2021

Dir. Łukasz Gutt, Łukasz Ronduda  ·  Retrospektywa: Łukasz Ronduda

Directed by Łukasz Ronduda and Łukasz Gutt (Poland, 2021, 90 min), the film is loosely based on the life of Daniel Rycharski, a real folk artist from a small Polish village who is openly gay and a practicing Catholic. Dawid Ogrodnik plays Daniel, who decorates his neighbors' homes with murals, exhibits sculptures in Warsaw galleries, and builds a chapel incorporating residents' recorded voices, wearing a hoodie with rainbow details as a personal signature. When events in his community escalate, he is pushed to publicly confront homophobia and argue that the Catholic Church should not exclude anyone. The film won the Golden Lions for Best Film at the 2021 Gdynia Film Festival, along with prizes for cinematography and acting.

13:00 Zatoka Brazilian observational documentary about Rio de Janeiro's Guanabara Bay, told through vignettes of the people who work and live along its shores.

A Bay

13:00–15:14 KNH 5 2h 14m Brazylia, 2021

Dir. Murilo Salles  ·  Lost Lost Lost

Director Murilo Salles spent four years filming dockworkers, fishermen, shellfish gatherers and other residents whose lives are tied to Guanabara Bay, distilling the footage into eight episodes each named for a different part of the bay (Ilha do Governador, Niterói, Paquetá, São Gonçalo, Maré, etc.). Shot in long, carefully composed static takes with an unconventional, non-naturalistic soundtrack, the film sets the bay's natural beauty against the social inequality and industrial decay of Bolsonaro-era Brazil. It premiered at DOK Leipzig in 2021 and won awards for direction/editing at the Rio de Janeiro Film Festival. Part of NH's 'Lost Lost Lost' section for observational/essayistic nonfiction.

Directed by Jerzy Skolimowski and starring Jeremy Irons, the film (original title "Moonlighting") follows four Polish laborers who travel to London to renovate a flat for cash, working without permits. Nowak (Irons), the only one who speaks English, learns that martial law has just been declared in Poland and conceals the news from his coworkers to keep them focused on finishing the job and earning money. It won the Best Screenplay award at the 1982 Cannes Film Festival.

21:30 Monty Python i Święty Graal Repertory screening of the classic 1975 Monty Python comedy, a low-budget parody of the King Arthur legend.

Monty Python and the Holy Grail

21:30–23:01 Zamek 1h 31m Wielka Brytania, 1975

Dir. Terry Jones, Terry Gilliam  ·  OPT Zamek w Leśnicy

This is a retrospective screening of the well-known 1975 British comedy, not a new film. Directed by Terry Jones and Terry Gilliam and written by and starring the Monty Python troupe (Graham Chapman, John Cleese, Gilliam, Eric Idle, Jones, Michael Palin), it follows King Arthur and his knights on a low-budget, absurdist quest for the Holy Grail, spoofing Arthurian legend and medieval-epic film conventions. Shown as an open-air screening in NH's 'OPT Zamek w Leśnicy' (Leśnica Castle open-air cinema) programme, on Sunday, August 2 at 21:30.
